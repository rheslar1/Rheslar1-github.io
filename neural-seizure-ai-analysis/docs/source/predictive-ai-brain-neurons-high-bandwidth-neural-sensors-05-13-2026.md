# Predictive AI Analysis of Brain Neurons Using High‑Bandwidth Neural Sensors for Early Detection of Brain Seizures.

Robert F. Heslar, Senior Firmware Engineer

April 5, 2026

### Abstract

Early detection of epileptic seizures remains a critical challenge in computational neuroscience. High‑bandwidth neural sensors—such as intracortical microelectrode arrays, high‑density electrocorticography (ECOG), and advanced EEG systems—enable for the capture of fine‑grained neuronal dynamics that precede seizure onset. When paired with predictive artificial intelligence (AI), these sensors support real‑time identification of pre‑ictal biomarkers. This manuscript presents a comprehensive evaluation of predictive AI methodologies applied to high‑resolution neural data, including deep learning architectures, multimodal signal fusion, knowledge distillation (KD), and closed‑loop neuromodulation. Results from recent peer‑reviewed studies demonstrate that convolutional neural networks, recurrent architectures, and transformer‑based models significantly outperform traditional statistical methods. KD further enables deployment of high‑accuracy models on embedded neurotechnology platforms. Ethical considerations, technical limitations, and future research directions are discussed. The integration of predictive AI with high‑bandwidth neural interfaces represents a transformative pathway toward personalized seizure forecasting and next‑generation neurotherapeutics.

## 1. Introduction

Epileptic seizures arise from abnormal, synchronous neuronal firing that disrupts normal brain function. For individuals with drug‑resistant epilepsy, early detection of seizure onset is essential for enabling timely interventions and improving quality of life. Traditional EEG systems provide valuable diagnostic information but lack the spatial and temporal resolution required to capture micro‑scale neuronal events linked to seizure onset.

High‑bandwidth neural sensors address this limitation by enabling kilohertz‑level sampling of neuronal activity. These sensors generate large, complex datasets that require advanced AI models for interpretation. Recent studies demonstrate that deep learning models can reliably predict seizures using intracranial EEG (iEEG), high‑density EEG, and ECoG. A 2026 Nature Scientific Reports study achieved 94–98% sensitivity using a lightweight MobileNet‑derived architecture on clinical EEG datasets. Systematic reviews from Springer, MDPI, and Frontiers confirm the rapid evolution of neural network architectures—including CNNs, LSTMs, graph neural networks, and transformers—for seizure prediction.

This manuscript evaluates predictive AI models applied to high‑bandwidth neural data, presenting a structured analysis of methods, results, and implications for clinical neurotechnology.
